# The Command Suite — full-site audit and optimization pass

Date: September 11, 2026

## Executive assessment

The site has a credible, differentiated foundation. Its strongest assets are the interactive workflow demonstration, explicit human-approval model, honest integration language, clear founding-pilot terms, separate Permit Command offer, and refusal to invent testimonials or savings. The highest-impact remaining work was not adding more pages; it was reducing search duplication, making the core workflow consistent everywhere, sharpening the first-screen promise, and aligning form CTAs with what the form actually does.

## Locked positioning

The Command Suite is a controlled field-to-office workflow service for owner-led contractors and small teams. It captures updates crews already send by text, voice note, email, photo, or document; structures the source into a usable draft; requests only missing details; and holds consequential routing for human approval. It complements existing accounting and project systems rather than replacing them.

Permit Command is a separate, human-accountable permit-coordination service. It is not presented as part of the $79 workflow pilot.

## Corrected message hierarchy

1. **Primary outcome:** Turn scattered field updates into records the office can use.
2. **How it works:** Capture → Structure → Clarify → Approve + Route.
3. **What it handles:** change orders, daily logs, receipts, job photos, and other scoped field-to-office handoffs.
4. **What it is not:** a CRM, accounting platform, project-management replacement, or autonomous system making unapproved changes.
5. **Proof:** show the workflow, source preservation, approval point, and route. Add customer proof only after real permission is obtained.
6. **Offer:** one defined workflow, one intake channel, one approver, one practical destination route, guided activation, and a 30-day review for $79/month.

## Changes implemented

- Rewrote the homepage hero around the customer's operational outcome rather than the generic idea of “losing profitable work.”
- Made the short homepage explanation use the exact four-stage product workflow.
- Changed solution-page calls to action from “Book” to “Request” because the destination is an application form, not a live calendar.
- Removed “low-cost” from search and social descriptions to preserve the luxury, high-control positioning.
- Added permanent redirects from three overlapping search pages to the strongest canonical pages.
- Removed those duplicate URLs from the sitemap and refreshed the homepage modification date.
- Expanded the workflow strip to four responsive stages while preserving the established blush, gold, warm-white, taupe, and charcoal brand system.

## SEO findings

### Resolved

- Duplicate-intent page clusters were competing for the same searches. Permanent redirects now consolidate authority:
  - `/construction-daily-log-automation` → `/contractor-daily-logs`
  - `/contractor-change-order-workflow` → `/construction-change-orders`
  - `/construction-receipt-tracking` → `/contractor-receipt-capture`
- Canonical domain remains `https://thecommandsuite.com`.
- `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, page metadata, Organization schema, WebSite schema, and SoftwareApplication schema are present.

### Ongoing

- After publication, resubmit `https://thecommandsuite.com/sitemap.xml` in Google Search Console and request recrawling of the homepage and primary solution pages.
- Search Console performance should be reviewed after enough impressions accumulate; do not rewrite pages based on a few days of data.
- Add FAQ structured data only if the visible answers remain identical to the schema.

## Conversion findings

### Strong

- The $79 pilot scope, activation timing, cancellation method, refund boundary, and support channel are plainly described.
- The site asks prospects to name one broken handoff, which is the correct sales conversation.
- The product demonstration is specific and does not masquerade as a testimonial.

### Next evidence milestone

The single most valuable future addition is one consented, real pilot story showing:

1. the anonymized original field message;
2. the structured record;
3. the clarification requested;
4. the human approval; and
5. the delivered result.

Do not add a quote, customer logo, percentage, time-saved claim, or scarcity count without verifiable permission and current evidence.

## UX and accessibility findings

- The site already has a skip link, visible keyboard focus, explicit form labels, reduced-motion handling, responsive layouts, and meaningful founder-image alt text.
- The four-stage strip collapses to a single readable column on smaller screens.
- Gold is used for accents and large display text, not relied on as low-contrast body copy.
- Continue testing at 200% browser zoom and on a real phone after major layout changes.

## Technical accuracy guardrails

- Preserve original source material when the selected workflow supports it.
- Require a named human approval point before consequential routing.
- Describe QuickBooks Online, Housecall Pro, JobTread, and Buildertrend routes as scoped compatibility or assisted routes unless a native integration has been verified.
- Do not promise universal retry behavior, delivery receipts, retention periods, certifications, or integrations that have not been implemented and tested for the customer's route.

## Measurement plan

Track these events as one funnel:

1. walkthrough started and completed;
2. sample record downloaded or emailed;
3. pilot-fit CTA clicked;
4. pilot-fit form started and submitted;
5. approved checkout link clicked;
6. paid onboarding submitted;
7. Command Inbox opened;
8. first workflow completed.

Review conversion by source and device. Optimize the largest verified drop-off first.
